**Caveat** - In the context of development and operation, a "caveat" refers to a warning or a specific stipulation, condition, or limitation that should be considered or heeded. It highlights potential issues, risks, or important factors that might affect the development process or the operation of a system, project, or procedure. Caveats are crucial in ensuring that all stakeholders are aware of possible pitfalls or constraints, allowing them to make informed decisions and take appropriate precautions.

**Smoke Test** - In the context of development and operations (DevOps), a **smoke test** is a preliminary set of tests run on a new build to ensure that the most critical functionalities of the software are working correctly. The main purpose of a smoke test is to verify that the build is stable enough for further testing and development. Here are some key points about smoke testing:

1. **Basic Functionality**: Smoke tests cover the most basic and critical functions of the application. If these basic functions work, the build is considered "smoke tested" and ready for more detailed testing.

2. **Early Detection**: It helps in identifying major issues early in the development cycle, preventing the team from wasting time on a build that has fundamental problems.

3. **Quick and Simple**: Smoke tests are usually quick and simple to execute. They are not exhaustive but are designed to quickly assess the health of the build.

4. **Automated**: In a DevOps environment, smoke tests are often automated to run as part of the continuous integration/continuous deployment (CI/CD) pipeline. This ensures that every new build undergoes smoke testing before it progresses further down the pipeline.

5. **Pass/Fail Criteria**: The result of a smoke test is typically binary: pass or fail. If the build passes the smoke test, it can move on to more comprehensive testing. If it fails, the build is rejected, and the issues must be resolved before further testing or deployment.

Smoke testing ensures that only stable builds are subjected to more rigorous and detailed testing, improving the efficiency of the development and deployment process.
